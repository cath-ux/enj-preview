Homepage directions

Three working homepages built on the same strategy, the same real photography and the same verified facts. Only the design thinking changes, so the comparison is about direction rather than copy. Our recommendation is marked.

Option A

Closing Day

Our recommendation

The heavy-machinery direction from the first reference, carrying the closing-day story. Condensed display caps over full-bleed equipment photography, a hazard-orange system, a scrolling town ticker, a draggable job carousel, and the failed-inspection rescue band front and center.

Why we recommend it. It is the only direction built on the one thing the research validated as this business's differentiator, and it is the direction that turns traffic into calls fastest.

Open Option A
  • FeelDark industrial, hazard orange, condensed caps.
  • Leads withThe failed inspection and the closing date.
  • PhotographyFull-bleed hero, five-job carousel, field and portrait plates.
  • Best ifThe priority is converting the homeowner who is already panicking.
Option B

The Ground Beneath

The corporate-grid direction from the second reference. A soil cross-section still anchors the hero, but the page now runs on a construction-orange system: a full-orange stats band, a six-cell icon services grid, and a Built to Last project card row over real job photos.

Trade-off. The most conventional read of the three, which is also its strength: it looks like an established commercial contractor at first glance. The soil diagram keeps it from being generic.

Open Option B
  • FeelDark corporate, construction orange, engineered grids.
  • Leads withSussex County geology and N.J.A.C. 7:9A.
  • PhotographyAnchored by a treated hero photo and one full-bleed field plate; still the most type-led.
  • Best ifThe goal is authority and differentiation against larger, older competitors.
Option C

Master Excavator

The vintage-trade direction from the third reference. A dark sepia hero with a duotone portrait and a handwritten script lead-in, five circular credential badges on the dark band, a trade-ledger body on textured paper, and a warm brown contact band to close.

Trade-off. The warmest and most human of the three. It competes on accountability rather than tenure, which is deliberate since rivals here have forty to sixty years.

Open Option C
  • FeelVintage trade, sepia and script, hand-set ledger.
  • Leads withOwner operated, and the promise not to oversell.
  • PhotographyMedium. One large portrait plus one site shot.
  • Best ifThe goal is trust and referral, and the owner is comfortable being the face.

What is shared across all three

These are deliberately identical so the comparison tests design, not copy.

  • Same phone throughout, (973) 702-0655, and the Wantage address.
  • Tenure stated as nearly 20 years.
  • No septic pumping anywhere, no emergency or same-day claims, no guarantees.
  • The 4.9 Google rating shown was verified in the July 2026 audit pull, 48 reviews at that time. No invented quotes or testimonials anywhere.
  • Sparta framed as organic service coverage only, never as a map result.
  • Only the client's own photographs. The one rendering is labelled as an illustration.
